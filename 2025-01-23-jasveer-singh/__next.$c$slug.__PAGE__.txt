<p>Is Einstein's theory of gravity correct? If so, let's take it to its most extreme. We dive into the strong-field regime during a black hole merger - the most extreme environments where gravity reigns supreme, to test the limits of Einstein's gravity.  We see how the event horizons evolve during a merger and how non-linear effects help to correlate these changes to gravitational waves that we can measure. We prove why these must be dominated by "quasi-normal modes" and how the non-linear effects will look like in them.</p>
</div>
<p>We take an analytical approach to calculate the non-linear solutions, especially near the event horizon and calculate these non-linearities and see how well they stand up to results from simulations and observations from LIGO (they do :))</p>
<p>We see how black holes form and evolve, what do merger events look like, what happens near the event horizon and how does research in this field look like.
